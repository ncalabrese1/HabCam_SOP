13  Frame Schematics & Physical Envelope

13.0.1 Structural Core & Design Principles

  • Primary Frame Architecture: Built from 304 Stainless Steel welded tubular/pipe roll-cage construction for high tensile strength, impact toughness, and chassis rigidity[cite: 8].
  • Corrosion Mitigation: Exposed stainless steel structures utilize sacrificial zinc/aluminum anodes and marine-grade anti-corrosion coatings to combat pitting and galvanic action[cite: 8].
  • Fasteners & Hardware: Passivated 316/304 Stainless Steel hardware with anti-seize provisions prevents thread galling during deck maintenance[cite: 8].
  • Hydrodynamic Tow Envelope: Engineered to minimize roll and drag at 5–7 knot tow speeds approximately 2 meters above the seabed[cite: 8].

13.1 HabCam Frame Schematics

13.1.1 1. System Overview

This slide contains the structural schematics and technical drawings for the frame of the Habitat Mapping Camera System (HabCam)[cite: 8]. The HabCam is an advanced, continuously towed underwater imaging vehicle utilized for high-resolution benthic habitat and marine population surveys[cite: 8]. The frame serves as the foundational chassis, providing structural integrity and spatial orientation for a multi-million-dollar suite of oceanographic sensors[cite: 8].

13.1.2 2. Core Design

Because the HabCam operates just meters above the seafloor in challenging marine environments, its frame is engineered to meet strict operational tolerances[cite: 8]. The core design prioritizes three major factors[cite: 8]: * Hydrodynamic Stability: The geometry of the frame is designed to minimize drag and roll, ensuring a smooth, predictable flight path while being towed via the ship’s winch[cite: 8]. * Durability and Impact Resistance: Constructed from marine grade, corrosion resistant alloys, the frame features a robust roll-cage architecture[cite: 8]. This protects sensitive internal housings from catastrophic damage in the event of an accidental seafloor strike or boulder impact[cite: 8]. * Modularity and Accessibility: The frame must allow for rapid on deck maintenance[cite: 8]. The open architecture design ensures that technical teams (such as the HabCam Lead) can easily access fiber optic terminations, adjust wiring if necessary, and calibrate sensors between deployments[cite: 8].

13.1.3 3. Payload Integration

These schematics detail the mounting points, brackets, and load-bearing structures required to securely integrate the vehicle’s primary payload[cite: 8]. When reviewing the drawings, note the specific integration zones for[cite: 8]: * Primary and secondary high-resolution camera housings[cite: 8]. * Illumination strobes and associated power manifolds[cite: 8]. * Forward-looking obstacle avoidance sonar and altimeters[cite: 8]. * The primary tow-point assembly and fiber optic telemetry housing[cite: 8].

13.1.4 4. Navigating The Schematics

The slide begins with complete, fully assembled isometric views, followed by detailed top, side, and front elevations[cite: 8]. Subsequent sections break down individual sub-assemblies (e.g., the tow-arm mechanism, sensor brackets) and conclude with a comprehensive Bill of Materials (BOM) specifying required alloys, fasteners, and torque tolerances[cite: 8].

13.1.5 5. Materials

To maintain structural integrity during tow operations and cushion mechanical impact, the HabCam chassis and mounting components use the following specifications[cite: 8]: * Primary Structural Frame: 304 Stainless Steel tubular/pipe construction, selected for its high tensile strength, impact toughness, and weldability in structural roll-cage configurations[cite: 8]. * Corrosion Mitigation: Exposed 304 stainless steel components are equipped with sacrificial zinc/aluminum anodes or marine-grade anti-corrosion coatings to protect against pitting and galvanic action in prolonged saltwater immersion[cite: 8]. * Fasteners and Brackets: Passivated 316 / 304 Stainless Steel hardware with anti-seize provisions to prevent galling during on-deck servicing, or storage[cite: 8]. * Pressure Housings: Hard-anodized aluminum or specialized pressure-rated housings for sensitive cameras, strobes, and oceanographic sensors[cite: 8].
