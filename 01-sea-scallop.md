## Overview & Population Status

The primary Atlantic sea scallop fishery operates along the Atlantic coast from the Mid-Atlantic to the US/Canada border using predominantly paired or single scallop dredges and trawl gear[cite: 9]. Most vessels land scallops as shucked meats (the adductor muscle), though some land whole scallops[cite: 9]. Implementing regulations are found at **50 CFR part 648 subpart D**[cite: 9].

According to the 2020 stock assessment, Atlantic sea scallop is **not overfished** and **not subject to overfishing**[cite: 9].

<br>

## Appearance & Biology

* **Appearance:** Scallops are bivalves held together by an adductor muscle[cite: 9]. Shells are saucer-shaped with smooth, scalloped edges[cite: 9]. The upper shell is reddish-pink/brown and the lower shell is white or cream (5–10% are albinos)[cite: 9].
* **Lifespan:** Can live up to 20 years, growing rapidly during their first few years up to ~6 inches[cite: 9].
* **Reproduction:** Reproduce by age 2, producing hundreds of millions of eggs per year during late summer/early fall or spring spawning[cite: 9]. Larvae remain in the water column for 4–6 weeks before settling[cite: 9].
* **Locomotion:** Propel themselves through the water by snapping top and bottom shells open and shut to escape predators like sea stars[cite: 9].

## Habitat, Range & Management

Atlantic sea scallops are found in the Northwest Atlantic Ocean from Newfoundland to Cape Hatteras, NC[cite: 9]. Adult scallops live in dense "beds" on sandy or gravelly ocean floors at depths of 100 to 300 feet on Georges Bank and the Mid-Atlantic[cite: 9].

NOAA Fisheries and the New England Fishery Management Council manage the fishery under the **Atlantic Sea Scallop Fishery Management Plan** via Total Allowable Catch (TAC), crew limits, rotational closures, Vessel Monitoring Systems (VMS), and Individual Fishing Quotas (IFQs)[cite: 9].

<br>

## Harvest & Gear Impacts

In 2023, commercial landings totaled **27.4 million pounds** of meats valued at **$360 million**[cite: 9]. Fishermen primarily use turtle deflector-style dredges equipped with 4-inch rings to increase efficiency and allow undersized scallops to escape[cite: 9].
