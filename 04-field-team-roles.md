# Field Team Roles & Expectations

## 1. Shift Structure & Overview

**Cruise Manning Structure & Shift Operations:** The HabCam scientific party typically consists of 9 to 10 personnel operating on 24-hour continuous sea trials divided into two 12-hour watch rotations (e.g., 11:30 AM to 11:30 PM and 11:30 PM to 11:30 AM)[cite: 7]. Each shift is managed by a Watch Chief and includes a rotating team of Pilots, Co-Pilots, and Annotators who cycle stations every 30 to 60 minutes to prevent screen fatigue[cite: 7]. The Chief Scientist maintains active oversight across both watches (6 AM to 6 PM baseline plus as-needed response)[cite: 7].

| **Population Dynamics** | 1–2 People | Quantitative scientist; collaborates on trackline design, executes camera calibrations, verifies altimeter confidence, and synthesizes population data[cite: 7]. |

## 3. Detailed Role Specifications

### Chief Scientist
As the Chief Scientist, you act as the primary liaison between the scientific party, the vessel's crew, and shore-based support[cite: 7]. You must be prepared to make decisive, on-the-spot decisions at any time of day or night to ensure the success and safety of the mission[cite: 7].

### HabCam Lead
As the HabCam Lead, you are the primary technical authority responsible for the deployment, operation, and data integrity of the imaging system[cite: 7]. You manage both the physical hardware of the vehicle and the IT infrastructure required to process its data[cite: 7].

### Watch Chief
As a Watch Chief, you are the direct shift supervisor responsible for managing the personnel, operations, and data collection during your assigned rotation[cite: 7]. You ensure that scientific objectives are executed safely while maintaining data logging standards[cite: 7].

### Pilot
You are directly responsible for the safe, precise, and active underwater navigation of the HabCam vehicle[cite: 7]. You operate in short, 30- to 60-minute focused intervals to maintain sharp reflexes[cite: 7].

### Co-Pilot
Acts as the technical support partner and second pair of eyes for the Pilot to ensure flight safety, sonar obstacle analysis, and sensor telemetry health[cite: 7].
